Kubernetes Deployment Failures

When an application deployment fails, first inspect the deployment and its pods.
Use kubectl get pods to identify pods that are Pending, Error, or CrashLoopBackOff.
Use kubectl describe pod to inspect scheduling problems, failed mounts, image pull
errors, and container configuration.

For application failures, inspect container logs using kubectl logs. Do not restart
a deployment until the cause of the failure has been investigated. Production
restart operations require human approval.

PostgreSQL Recovery

If PostgreSQL becomes unavailable, first determine whether the database process is
running and whether the storage volume is accessible. Check PostgreSQL logs before
attempting recovery.

A database must not be deleted or reinitialised as an initial recovery action.
Before any destructive recovery operation, verify that a valid backup exists and
obtain human approval.

Elasticsearch Red Cluster

A red Elasticsearch cluster indicates that at least one primary shard is
unassigned. First inspect cluster health and shard allocation.

Determine why the primary shard cannot be allocated before changing allocation
settings. Possible causes include unavailable nodes, disk capacity problems, and
allocation constraints.

AI Operational Safety

The AI operations assistant may inspect infrastructure, retrieve documentation,
analyse logs, and recommend remediation actions.

Read-only diagnostic operations may be executed automatically.

Changes to production infrastructure, service restarts, deletion of resources,
database recovery operations, and other destructive actions require explicit
human approval.
